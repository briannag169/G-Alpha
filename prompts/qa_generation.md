# Prompt Template: GPT-4 Q&A Pair Generation for Fine-Tuning

Used to generate instruction-following Q&A pairs from 10-K sections. These pairs form the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset for G-Alpha's domain-specific financial AI model.

---

## System Prompt

```
You are a financial domain expert specialising in SEC regulatory filings. Your task is to
generate high-quality question-and-answer pairs from excerpts of 10-K annual reports.

Each Q&A pair must:
- Be answerable solely from the provided text excerpt — no external knowledge
- Represent the type of question a financial analyst or institutional investor would ask
- Have a precise, factual answer that can be verified against the source text
- Cover different aspects of the excerpt (do not generate 5 questions about the same fact)

Question types to include (vary across the 5 pairs):
- Factual extraction ("What was the company's primary source of revenue?")
- Risk identification ("What regulatory risk did management identify?")
- Comparative/temporal ("How did the company's approach to X change compared to prior year?")
- Implication ("What does management's statement about X suggest about future capital allocation?")
- Numerical ("What percentage of revenue came from international markets?")

Format each pair as:
Q: <question>
A: <answer>
```

---

## User Prompt Template

```
Generate 5 question-and-answer pairs from the following 10-K section excerpt.

Company: {ticker}
Section: {section_name}
Filing Date: {filing_date}

---
{section_text}
---

Generate exactly 5 Q&A pairs. Follow the format:
Q: <question>
A: <answer>
```

**Variables:**
- `{ticker}` — uppercase equity ticker, e.g. `MSFT`
- `{section_name}` — human-readable section name, e.g. `Risk Factors (1A)`, `MD&A (7)`
- `{filing_date}` — ISO date string, e.g. `2022-06-30`
- `{section_text}` — raw section text retrieved via `get_historical_sections()`

---

## Example Input / Output

**Input:**

```
Company: MSFT
Section: Risk Factors (1A)
Filing Date: 2022-06-30

...Intense competition in all aspects of our business... We face formidable competition
across all of our businesses, including from companies that have entered markets historically
dominated by Microsoft... Our competitors range from established technology companies to
emerging startups with innovative approaches to delivering cloud services, productivity
tools, and gaming experiences...
```

**Output:**

```
Q: What types of competitors does Microsoft identify as sources of competitive risk?
A: Microsoft identifies competition from both established technology companies and emerging
startups with innovative approaches to delivering cloud services, productivity tools, and
gaming experiences.

Q: Which business segments does Microsoft consider subject to intense competitive pressure?
A: Microsoft describes all aspects of its business as facing intense competition, including
cloud services, productivity tools, and gaming.

Q: How does Microsoft characterise the competitive landscape relative to its historical
market position?
A: Microsoft acknowledges that competitors have entered markets historically dominated by
Microsoft, indicating an erosion of historical competitive barriers.

Q: What product categories are explicitly named as competitive battlegrounds in this excerpt?
A: Cloud services, productivity tools, and gaming experiences are explicitly named.

Q: What does Microsoft's description of competition from "emerging startups" imply about
its risk assessment methodology?
A: It implies Microsoft views disruption risk from non-incumbent players as material,
not just competition from established technology peers.
```

## Design Notes

**Why 5 pairs per excerpt?** Empirically, 5 pairs per section excerpt provides sufficient diversity without redundancy. Fewer than 3 pairs under-represent the section's content; more than 7 pairs tend to repeat the same factual anchors.

**Why "answerable solely from the provided text"?** This constraint ensures the fine-tuning signal teaches the model to reason from retrieved context, not from parametric memory — directly aligned with the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architecture the production agent uses.

**Temperature:** `0.3` — slightly above zero to introduce variation in question phrasing across different sections covering similar topics (e.g., all companies discuss competitive risk in 1A). Pure zero-temperature would generate near-identical questions for similar text, reducing dataset diversity.

**Max tokens:** `800` — sufficient for 5 Q&A pairs with substantive answers. Outputs beyond this length indicate the model is elaborating unnecessarily.

**Section selection for fine-tuning:** Sections 1A (Risk Factors), 7 (MD&A), and 8 (Financial Statements) are the highest-signal sections for financial analyst training. Section 1 (Business) and 11 (Executive Compensation) are lower priority but included for breadth.
